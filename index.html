How ResaleLedger works

The idea

One file, no account, no subscription, and — read this part twice — no connection to eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, Depop, or Whatnot. ResaleLedger does not crosslist, does not sync, and never asks for your platform logins. It's the bookkeeping half of reselling: what you paid, where it's listed, what it sold for, what the platform kept, and what actually landed in your pocket. You type a sale in about fifteen seconds; the math is the app's job.

Your true cost per item

Item cost = price paid + "extra cost to get it." The extra field is where a sourcing trip's gas, an estate-sale entry fee, or the shipping on a wholesale lot gets spread across the items it bought. That total is the COGS used when the item sells — and it's snapshotted onto the sale, so repricing or correcting the item later never rewrites a past sale.

Net profit on a sale

Profit = sale price + shipping charged to the buyer − platform fee − shipping you paid − other costs − item cost. The platform fee is the % you enter on the sale (pre-filled from Settings if you've set one) × the sale price, rounded to cents and stored with the sale. Shipping the buyer paid you counts as income; shipping you paid the carrier counts as a cost — if they cancel out, they cancel out honestly.

Returns

When a sale is marked returned, the whole sale reverses: price, shipping charged, fee, other costs, and COGS all come back off the books, and the item returns to stock. What doesn't come back is postage: a returned sale's net = − (outbound shipping you paid) − (return shipping you paid). That's how returns actually feel, because it's what they actually cost. If your platform kept part of its fee on a return, record that kept amount as an "other cost" on a fresh note in the sale's notes… or simpler: most platforms refund the fee on a full refund, which is the case this convention assumes — check your statement for big-ticket items.

Sell-through & days to sell

Sell-through = items sold in the period ÷ items acquired in the period. A return doesn't count as sold. Days to sell averages (sale date − date acquired) across completed sales. Both are shown for the period you pick in Money & Taxes.

Aging & the dead pile

Age counts from the date acquired for anything unsold. 90+ days gets flagged; 180+ days is the dead pile. The dollar figure next to it is your own cash sitting on a shelf — the report exists to make that number annoying enough to act on.

Fees are yours to enter — on purpose

Platform fees change with time, category, seller level, and promotions. Any app that hard-codes "eBay = 13.25%" is lying to you by spring. ResaleLedger ships the fee fields blank, asks you to copy the real % off your payout statements, and then keeps the % you confirmed frozen on each sale forever.

Your data

Everything is stored in this browser's local storage on this device only. Clearing your browser's site data erases it — export backups from Settings and keep the file somewhere safe. A backup from this app re-imports into any copy of ResaleLedger, and only ResaleLedger — it won't touch your other books.

Taxes

The tax-season summary organizes a year's records the way a Schedule C is organized: gross sales, cost of goods sold, platform fees, shipping, other costs, and net. It's bookkeeping, not tax advice, and not a return. Most resellers who sell regularly are a business in the IRS's eyes whether they file like one or not — a tax preparer is worth it, and this printout is what they'll ask for.
